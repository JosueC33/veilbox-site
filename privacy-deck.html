The whole policy, in one breath

No accounts. No analytics. No ads. No tracking. No crash reporting.

  • No account — there is nothing to sign up for, and nothing to sign in to
  • No analytics — the app does not measure you or phone home
  • No advertising and no ad networks
  • No tracking of any kind, across apps or anywhere else
  • No crash reporting — not even the kind most apps consider harmless
There is no record of you to hand over, sell, lose, or be compelled to produce.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything — unless you turn on iCloud backup

These are stored on your own device and are never sent anywhere:

  • Your photos and videos, encrypted with AES-256
  • Your vault code — stored only as a one-way hash, never in readable form
  • Your encryption keys, held in the iOS Keychain
  • Albums, dates, and app settings
3 / 8
If you turn on iCloud backup

It goes to your iCloud — and it is already unreadable when it leaves

Encrypted data is copied to a private database only your Apple Account can reach. We have no server and receive nothing.

Encrypted before it leaves

  • Your photos and videos
  • The metadata too — album membership, dates and deletion state are sealed, because how a vault is organised is itself revealing
  • Apple stores it and cannot read it. Neither can we.

Your code never goes up

  • What is uploaded is your encryption key wrapped under a key derived from your code — PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 rounds
  • Without the code it is unusable ciphertext
  • This is what lets the same code open your vault on a new phone — and why a forgotten code cannot be recovered by anyone

Permissions

Asked for narrowly, and only when you act

📷 Photo library Permission to read photos is requested only when you import — and only add-only permission when you export back to your camera roll. The app never browses your library on its own.
🎥 Camera Only if you enable the break-in log, which photographs whoever enters a wrong code. Those photos are encrypted in your vault and never leave your device unless backup is on. iOS always shows the green camera indicator when the camera is used — that cannot be turned off, and we would not want it to be.
💳 Purchases Handled entirely by Apple. We never see your payment details. Veilbox asks Apple whether a purchase is active, and nothing more.
5 / 8
Keeping and deleting your data

Almost none of it is ours to keep

On your iPhone Photos stay until you delete them. A deleted photo goes to Recently Deleted, is recoverable for 30 days, then is erased permanently — and you can empty that folder immediately from inside the app. Deleting the app removes everything it stored.
In iCloud FREE Only if you turned backup on, and only into your own account. Settings → Danger zone → Remove everything from iCloud deletes the cloud copy and turns backup off, leaving the photos on your phone untouched. No subscription required.
Getting your data out FREE Settings → Export photos saves everything, or one album, back to your camera roll. Always free — a subscription that lapsed can never strand your own photos.
Withdrawing permission Photo library and camera access can be revoked any time in iOS Settings under Veilbox. The app keeps working; it simply stops being able to import or capture.

What we hold about you

Nothing.

There is no account, no server, and no analytics — so there is no record of you to request the deletion of.

Veilbox is not directed at children, and collects no data from anyone.
